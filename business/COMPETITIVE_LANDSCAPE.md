## 6. Google Play & Android Platform Rules (Accessibility, Advanced Protection, Background Microphone, Fees, Distribution)

Raw report: `research/05_platform_incumbents_policy_risk_market.md`, Part B

| Rule | What it says | Status |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Accessibility API on Google Play** | Apps that are not accessibility tools may use it only with: documentation on the store listing, a prominent in-app disclosure, affirmative consent, and a Play Console declaration with a demo video. "Automation tools, assistants" are named as *not* accessibility tools. Apps that "autonomously initiate, plan, and execute actions" are prohibited. Undeclared use can end in suspension or account termination. | **V** |
| **Android 17 Advanced Protection** | When the user switches it on, Accessibility access is restricted to verified accessibility tools (Google's own blog, 1 Oct 2026). It is opt-in. | **V** |
| **Background microphone service** | Android's documentation: a microphone foreground service cannot be *created* while the app is in the background, even under the usual exemptions. An accessibility key event is not among the exemptions. *(Engineering question — whether Eudemonia's already-running service satisfies this on every phone — not a business fact.)* | **V** |
| **Per-app memory limits in Android 17** | Introduced on Pixel first; an app over the limit is slowed and may be terminated. No limit values published; treatment of foreground services and memory-mapped files not stated. *(Engineering question.)* | **V** |
| **Developer verification** | Applies to all apps on certified devices "regardless of download source". Live in Brazil, Indonesia, Singapore, Thailand from 30 Sep 2026; global in 2027. Google Play developers are registered automatically. | **V** |
| **Google Play fees (US, UK, EEA, Australia, Japan; from 30 Jun 2026)** | Subscriptions: 10% + 5% billing fee = **15%**. One-time and other purchases on new installs: 20% + 5% = **25%**. Elsewhere: 15% on the first $1M a year. | **V** |
| **Large model delivery** | "Play for On-device AI": packs up to 1.5 GB each, can be targeted by device memory. A 2.5 GB model needs two packs. | **V** |
| Battery-optimisation exemption | Play permits the direct request only where the core function breaks without it; voice capture is not on the listed examples. | P |
| Real-world Accessibility cases | Button-remapping and automation apps exist on Play; one solo developer reported four rejections for a missing disclosure. Current store status of the best-known apps could not be confirmed. | U |

## 7. How Local-First Software Makes Money (Precedents & Licensing Without Accounts)

Raw report: `research/04_local_first_monetization_precedents.md`

| Model | Example | Status |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Free app + paid service that runs on a server (sync, publishing) | Obsidian, Joplin Cloud, Standard Notes | P |
| Fixed one-time price on the honour system, no licence check | **FUTO Keyboard**: $6.99 direct, $11.99 on Google Play; "does not have a license key verification system due to being offline"; payment notice after 30 days | **V** |
| One-time licence | VoiceInk $25–49, MacWhisper €64, Private LLM | P |
| Free + one-time feature unlock | PocketPal | P |
| Local free + cloud subscription | Enclave, Anarlog | P |

- Every local-first product found with **recurring** income charges for something that has a server cost. No product was found selling a subscription for purely local processing with no server feature. (Researcher's synthesis; consistent with every verified row.)
- FUTO Voice Input, the closest Android on-device voice precedent, shows about **63K installs** on a store tracker. (U)
- No data exists anywhere on what share of users pay under an honour system.
- **Backlash cases** (Notability 2021, Voice Dream 2024): reversals followed the removal of features from people who had already paid. Charging new buyers differently (Screen Studio 2025) drew criticism but no reversal. (P)
- Paying after value has been received raises what people pay (academic work, reported by press). (P)
- **Google Play billing without a server**: Google's guidance assumes a backend; purchases must be acknowledged within three days or they are refunded; purchases restore through the buyer's Google account. Whether restore works fully offline, and whether the billing component adds an internet permission to the app, are **not verified**.
- Google Play has no mechanism for charging existing owners for a major new version; a new in-app purchase is the workaround. (U — inference)

## 8. Subscription Benchmarks (Conversion, Retention, Android Against iPhone)

Raw report: `research/04_local_first_monetization_precedents.md`. Sources: RevenueCat and Adapty 2026 reports. All **P**; the reports cover subscription apps only and say little about one-time pricing. Some figures conflict inside the reports themselves.

- Install-to-paid, freemium apps: about **2%** median at day 35; hard paywall about 10%.
- AI apps: more revenue per payer, faster churn; yearly-plan retention at 12 months about **21%** (28% across all apps).
- Android: about **15%** of subscription revenue against 85% for iPhone, with about 70% of users. Per paying user the first-year value is close ($21.62 against $23.38). The gap is in how many pay, about three times fewer.
- Median prices: $10 a month, about $35 a year.
- Productivity apps: highest refund rate of any category (4.7%); 44% of Google Play cancellations are cost-related.
- Lifetime is usually priced at several times the annual plan; about a quarter of apps offer subscription and lifetime together.

## 9. Market Size, Capable Devices & Willingness to Pay (Estimates and Surveys)

Raw report: `research/05_platform_incumbents_policy_risk_market.md`, Part C. All **U** unless marked.

- AI note-taking market: estimates of $0.6–1.1B for 2025, differing by about two times between research firms.
- Phone memory: average 8.4 GB at the end of 2025 (Counterpoint). The only breakdown found (a benchmark app's users, skewed to enthusiasts) shows 82% at 8 GB or more and 43% at 12 GB or more. **No figure exists for phones actually in use.** A 2026 memory shortage is cutting shipments and pushing mid-range phones back to 8 GB.
- Premium phones ($600+): Apple holds over 70% of units. Pixel is about 1% of global shipments; Samsung 24%.
- Willingness to pay for AI on phones (US survey, 2025): 3% would pay extra; 50% would not.
- Privacy: about 40% of US users say they worry about AI and privacy on phones. No evidence was found that "no network" has driven purchases of a voice-capture product; the commercially successful ones are cloud products.

## 11. Price Level Against Conversion, Voluntary Payment & India Pricing (Benchmarks, Named Cases, Google Play Price Mechanics)

Raw report: `research/06_low_price_and_honour_system_evidence.md` (wave 2, 2026-11-25). **V** here means re-checked on 2026-11-25.

**Benchmarks across many apps (RevenueCat, State of Subscription Apps 2026 edition, data year 2025).** Price tiers are relative to other apps (below average / average / above average), not dollar bands, and the figures compare *different apps*; they do not show what happens when one app changes its price. **V**

| Median | Low-priced apps | Mid-priced | High-priced |
| :--- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Installs that pay within 35 days | 1.4% | 2.0% | 2.8% |
| Revenue per install after 60 days | $0.11 | — | $0.94 |
| Yearly plans still paying after one year | 36% | 26% | 23% |
| Refund rate (**P**) | 2.7% | 3.9% | 4.5% |

- India and South-East Asia (by user location): 0.7% of installs pay, the lowest of any region **V**; refund rate 7.7%, the highest; prices at about half of top-tier markets (**P**).
- In the 2025 edition's data, the cheapest quarter of monthly plans starts at $3.26 and the median is $6.68 (via a third party citing RevenueCat, **U**). $0.59–0.99 a month is far below the range these benchmarks describe.
- 2025 edition: among users who start a trial, low-priced apps convert more of them (47.8% against 28.4%) (**P**).

**Price tests on single apps.** No published test was found of a cut to about $1 a month or less. Published tests go the other way and are vendor case studies, likely selected for success (**P**): a 50% price rise cut conversions 19% and raised income per user 25% (BuyBye); a 43% rise raised income per user 47.5% (PropGPT); a cut from $59.99 to $44.99 a year raised income per user 10% (SellRaze).

**Named apps at about $1 a month.**
- **HabitKit** (habit tracker, solo founder, iPhone and Android): founder reports for 2025 total revenue $602,000, monthly recurring revenue $28,000, 25,100 active subscribers, 272,000 App Store and 290,000 Google Play downloads (**V** that the founder states this; the figures themselves are a founder claim). US App Store prices today: $0.99, $1.99 and $4.99 a month **V**. Interviews say the price began at $0.99 a month, was raised to $2 on iPhone, and that a price test on Android "failed"; no numbers published (**P**). Release year not established.
- **WhatsApp** charged $0.99 a year and dropped the fee in January 2016; its founder said "It really doesn't work that well" **V**.
- **Bitwarden** raised its personal plan from $10 to $19.80 a year in January 2026 (**P**).

**Voluntary payment.** Thunderbird's appeal page is titled "Only 3% of users support Thunderbird" (in the page title only; method not given) — partly confirmed. FUTO publishes no payer count. Pay-what-you-want experiments report 38–45% of *buyers* paying something, which is not comparable with a free app's whole user base (**U**). No in-app tip-jar result for any named independent app was found.

**India.**
- No named consumer app was found at or below ₹29 a month on a renewing monthly plan. Reference points (company pages and App Store India, **P**): Google One Lite ₹59, Truecaller ₹99, HabitKit from ₹89, Spotify ₹139, Netflix Mobile ₹149, ChatGPT Go ₹399, Todoist ₹330 a month.
- Recurring payments: India's central bank requires a notice to the customer at least 24 hours before each recurring debit, with no exemption found for small amounts (press report of the 2026 framework, **P**). One press report says 55–90% of UPI Autopay debits failed in August 2025 (**U**, snippet only).
- Tax: India is a tax-inclusive price market on Google Play **V**. Google collects and remits GST on behalf of developers located *outside* India; developers located *in* India handle GST themselves — partly confirmed; the exact net per sale for an India-based developer was not established.

**Google Play price mechanics.**
- Raising a subscription price affects new buyers only; existing subscribers stay on their old price by default ("legacy price cohort") **V**.
- Play Console's built-in price experiments cover one-time products only, not subscriptions **V**.
- Price floor: Google announced in May 2022 that products can be priced "as low as the equivalent of 5 US cents in any market" **V**. The current official minimum-price table could not be opened; $0.59 and ₹19 are above every floor figure found.

## 12. Capture Entry Points & Store Launch Mechanics (Accessibility Precedents on Google Play, Closed-Testing Rule, Lock-Screen Routes on Android and iPhone)

Raw report: `research/07_capture_trigger_precedents_and_launch_mechanics.md` (wave 2, 2026-11-25). **V** here means re-checked on 2026-11-25. Google Play pages returned only their titles to both researcher and checker, so listing *text* comes from third-party mirrors.

**Apps on Google Play that read hardware buttons through the Accessibility API for a non-accessibility purpose.**
- Key Mapper (about 12M installs), Button Mapper (about 7M), Tasker and MacroDroid are listed and were updated in October–November 2026 — listing existence partly confirmed (page titles load); install counts, dates and disclosure wording from mirrors (**U**).
- **"Action Button-Voice Automation"** (`app.voice.automation`): a voice recorder started by a volume-key long-press through an accessibility service, advertised as recording on a locked screen; first released January 2026; about 267 installs. Listing exists (title loads); description from a mirror — partly confirmed. A second recorder, VolRec (about 3.2K installs), starts from volume keys by an unknown mechanism (**U**).
- No documented removal or forced feature removal of a hardware-button app since the 2021 declaration rules was found. This is absence of evidence: Reddit and XDA could not be searched directly.
- The only first-hand rejection account found is a screen-time app rejected four times, first for a missing prominent disclosure; rejections continued until old builds in every testing track were replaced (**P**, founder post).
- Google on permission declarations in general: "Your request may require up to several weeks to process", and the app or update stays in pending publication until reviewed **V**. No account was found of how long an Accessibility declaration takes.

**Launch requirements for a new Google Play developer.**
- Personal accounts created after 13 November 2023: a closed test with at least 12 testers opted in continuously for 14 days before applying for production; review of that application "usually takes seven days or less" **V**. Google's setup page heads the testing requirement "(Personal accounts only)"; no explicit exemption wording for organisation accounts was found. Organisation accounts need a D-U-N-S number (**P**).
- Registration fee $25, one time (**P**, official page seen by researcher).

**Android capture routes that need no Accessibility permission.**
- Android's documentation allows a microphone foreground service to start from the background when the user interacts with a **notification** or an **app widget**, or when the app provides the voice-assistant service. Quick-settings tiles are not on that list **V**.
- No official statement was found on whether any of these can start a recording **without unlocking**. A notification action can be marked as requiring unlock; otherwise "the OS will decide" (**P**, official reference page).
- Lock-screen widgets reached Pixel phones with Android 16 QPR2 (December 2025); other makers differ (**P**). Android's lock-screen note shortcut has not shipped publicly (**P**).
- Samsung's side-button double press can start its own Voice Recorder **V**; whether it works past the lock, and for other apps, was not established. Pixel Quick Tap can open an app (**P**).

**iPhone capture routes.**
- Action Button: iPhone 15 Pro and later, all iPhone 16 and 17 models (**U**, third-party list). Whisper Memos states its Action Button recording works "even when your phone is locked" **V** (that the vendor claims it). Just Press Record claims Lock Screen, Control Centre and Action Button recording (**P**). Letterly requires Face ID first (**P**).
- A July 2026 Apple developer-forum thread reports that starting a recording from the Action Button on a locked iPhone fails or asks for Face ID; no replies **V**. Whether a third-party app can reliably do it is **unresolved**.
- Apple's `AudioRecordingIntent` (iOS 18) requires a Live Activity for the whole recording (**P**; Apple's page would not load for the checker).

**Running a local language model on iPhone.**
- Private LLM says iPhones with 6 GB (12 Pro and later) run 3–4B models; an independent guide says 8 GB (15 Pro and later) (**P** / **U**).
- Apple restricts a background app's access to the graphics chip; an Apple engineer said in October 2025 that background access was iPad-only (**P**; Apple's page would not load for the checker). PocketPal unloads its model when it goes to the background (**P**). Whether processor-only inference can continue in the background was not established.

## 13. How Comparable Independent Apps Got Users & Base Rates for Reaching $10K a Month (Channels, Named Cases, Paid-Install Costs, Play Launch Tools)

Raw report: `research/08_user_acquisition_channels_and_time_to_revenue.md` (wave 3, 2026-11-25). **V** here means re-checked on 2026-11-25. Success figures are founders' own claims unless stated; they come from winners.

**Base rates (RevenueCat, State of Subscription Apps 2026; subscription apps, about three quarters of new launches on iPhone).**
- 17.3% of newly launched apps reach $1K a month and 4.6% reach $10K a month within two years; medians of 58 days to $1K and 109 days to $10K, with no statement of whether the medians count only apps that got there **V**.
- Installs that pay within 35 days: the report's data section gives a median of **2.6% on the App Store against 0.9% on Google Play**; its own summary gives "2.9% vs 2.6%". Revenue per install after 60 days: $0.42 App Store against $0.16 Google Play in the data section, $0.23 in the summary. **The report contradicts itself**; both versions seen by the checker **V**.
- Apps that ask for money up front convert 10.7% of installs against 2.1% for freemium (median, day 35, subscriptions only) **V**.

- Sponsorship prices: roughly $200–1,500 for a video on a 10–50K-subscriber YouTube channel; newsletters $10–150 per thousand readers (compiled estimates, **U**).

**Paid installs.** Reported cost per install ranges from $0.68 (Android, global, all categories) to $14.80 (iPhone, North America, productivity); sources conflict and none is a primary dataset for US productivity apps (**U**). No source tests payback for a one-time price of about $40. Arithmetic only: $3.40 an install at 0.9% paying is about $378 per buyer.

**Google Play launch tools.**
- Promo codes: up to 500 per quarter across all non-subscription promotions in an app **V**.
- Devices can be excluded by RAM size **V**; the console shows RAM as an attribute of the install base (**P**).
- Pre-registration lasts at most 90 days; store-listing experiments test icon, graphics, screenshots and descriptions with up to two variants (**P**, official pages seen by researcher).

- Lifetime-to-yearly ratios in apps that offer both run from about 1.7× to 4× (**P**).
- Without a lifetime option: Voicenotes, Day One, Tasks.org; Daylio may have dropped lifetime on Google Play (**U**).

**Evidence on one-time against subscription.**
- **No published conversion rate for one-time unlock apps was found anywhere.**
- Adapty: 14% of apps offer a lifetime plan; it brings 1% of total subscription revenue, 5.6% in the typical app that sells it **V**. One-time purchases were 10.32% of app revenue in 2025, up from 6.42% in 2023 **V**.
- RevenueCat 2026: apps whose best-selling plan is lifetime earn a median $0.19 per install after 14 days and $0.24 after 60 **V**. About one app in four offers a lifetime plan (**P**).
- Halide (2021): subscribers outnumbered one-time buyers almost five to one, yet one-time revenue was 39% of the total **V**.
- Notability moved from one-time to subscription in 2021 and met wide criticism; it brought a one-time plan back in 2026 (**P**).

**Trials for a one-time purchase.**
- Google Play offers only pre-order and discount offers for one-time products; there is no free-trial offer type **V**. A trial must be built inside the app.
- Apple: "Non-subscription apps may offer a free time-based trial period before presenting a full unlock option by setting up a Non-Consumable IAP item at Price Tier 0 that follows the naming convention: 'XX-day Trial.'" (Guideline 3.1.1) **V**.
- Named apps use time limits (7–15 days), usage limits (minutes or words per day or week) or feature limits (**P**). **No published result compares these designs for one-time apps.**

**Store terms.**
- Apple: 15% commission under the Small Business Program for developers with up to $1 million in proceeds in the prior year; $99 annual membership **V**.
- Google Play price experiments (one-time products only): at most two variants and a control; minimum detectable effect 5–50% (default 30%); confidence 70–99% (default 90%); at most six months; the console warns when volume is too low **V**.
- Whether Play Billing needs the app to hold the internet permission: Google's documentation is silent. Developer reports say the permission arrives through a bundled library and that billing works without it; nobody reported a full device test (**U**).

**Earning later from one-time buyers.** Examples found: a new major version sold again (Things 3 — mixed reactions); a separate new app (Procreate Dreams); a one-time app converted to a subscription (Notability, Plex — backlash and refund requests) (**P**). No 2025–2026 Google Play example of a paid add-on sold to lifetime buyers was found.

## 15. Google Play Submission Requirements That Affect a Launch Date (Review Time, Declarations, Restricted Permissions, Billing)

Checked directly against Google's pages on 2026-11-25 (no separate raw report).

- **Review time**: "For certain developer accounts, we'll take more time to thoroughly review your app… This may result in review times of up to seven days or longer in exceptional cases." The page does not say which accounts — partly confirmed.
- **Foreground-service declaration**: every foreground-service type used must be declared on the App content page with a description and a video showing the user triggering the feature; for the microphone type, "Show the user initiate the microphone actively recording audio" **V**.
- **Battery-optimisation exemption**: "Google Play policies prohibit apps from requesting direct exemption from Power Management features — Doze and App Standby — in Android 6.0 and above unless the core function of the app is adversely affected." The table of acceptable uses lists safety, task-automation, companion-device and (conditionally) messaging or calling apps; **there is no voice-recording row**. Play's abuse policy gives as a violation "apps that are not eligible for allowlisting and attempt to bypass system power management" — partly confirmed (no explicit rejection wording).
- **Privacy policy**: "You must link to a privacy policy on your store listing and within your app, even if your app doesn't access personal or sensitive user data" **V** (wording).
- **Data safety form**: "Even developers with apps that do not collect any user data must complete this form" **V**.
- **Payments profile**: needed to sell; the bank account must be in the profile's country; Google's India page warns "this process can take significant time to complete" — partly confirmed.
- **Target API level**: new apps and updates must target Android 16 (API level 36) **V**.
- **One-time product**: must be created and activated in Play Console; draft purchase options are not returned to the app **V**.
- **Billing library**: latest release 9.1.0 (2026-06-18). The deprecation table gives version 7 a deadline of 31 August 2026 (extension to 1 November 2026) and version 8 a deadline of 31 August 2027, so version 8 is the oldest still within its deadline (checker's inference from the table) — partly confirmed.
