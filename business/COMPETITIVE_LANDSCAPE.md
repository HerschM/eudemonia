## 1. Reading Guide (Verification Status Codes & Evidence Grades)

- **V** = independently re-checked on 2026-11-24 by a second agent against the company's or platform's own page.
- **P** = reported by press or the founder; seen by the researcher, not independently re-checked. Treat as likely, not certain.
- **U** = unverified: third-party estimate, aggregator listing, or a claim the checker could not confirm.
- Prices are US list prices on the date above. Re-verify anything older than three months before relying on it.

## 6. Google Play & Android Platform Rules (Accessibility, Advanced Protection, Background Microphone, Fees, Distribution)

Raw report: `research/05_platform_incumbents_policy_risk_market.md`, Part B

| Rule | What it says | Status |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Accessibility API on Google Play** | Apps that are not accessibility tools may use it only with: documentation on the store listing, a prominent in-app disclosure, affirmative consent, and a Play Console declaration with a demo video. "Automation tools, assistants" are named as *not* accessibility tools. Apps that "autonomously initiate, plan, and execute actions" are prohibited. Undeclared use can end in suspension or account termination. | **V** |
| **Android 17 Advanced Protection** | When the user switches it on, Accessibility access is restricted to verified accessibility tools (Google's own blog, 1 Oct 2026). It is opt-in. | **V** |
| **Background microphone service** | Android's documentation: a microphone foreground service cannot be *created* while the app is in the background, even under the usual exemptions. An accessibility key event is not among the exemptions. *(Engineering question — whether Eudemonia's already-running service satisfies this on every phone — not a business fact.)* | **V** |
| **Per-app memory limits in Android 17** | Introduced on Pixel first; an app over the limit is slowed and may be terminated. No limit values published; treatment of foreground services and memory-mapped files not stated. *(Engineering question.)* | **V** |
| **Developer verification** | Applies to all apps on certified devices "regardless of download source". Live in Brazil, Indonesia, Singapore, Thailand from 30 Sep 2026; global in 2027. Google Play developers are registered automatically. | **V** |
| **Google Play fees (US, UK, EEA, Australia, Japan; from 30 Jun 2026)** | Subscriptions: 10% + 5% billing fee = **15%**. One-time and other purchases on new installs: 20% + 5% = **25%**. Elsewhere: 15% on the first $1M a year. | **V** |
| **Large model delivery** | "Play for On-device AI": packs up to 1.5 GB each, can be targeted by device memory. A 2.5 GB model needs two packs. | **V** |
| Battery-optimisation exemption | Play permits the direct request only where the core function breaks without it; voice capture is not on the listed examples. | P |
| Real-world Accessibility cases | Button-remapping and automation apps exist on Play; one solo developer reported four rejections for a missing disclosure. Current store status of the best-known apps could not be confirmed. | U |

## 7. How Local-First Software Makes Money (Precedents & Licensing Without Accounts)

Raw report: `research/04_local_first_monetization_precedents.md`

| Model | Example | Status |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Free app + paid service that runs on a server (sync, publishing) | Obsidian, Joplin Cloud, Standard Notes | P |
| Fixed one-time price on the honour system, no licence check | **FUTO Keyboard**: $6.99 direct, $11.99 on Google Play; "does not have a license key verification system due to being offline"; payment notice after 30 days | **V** |
| One-time licence | VoiceInk $25–49, MacWhisper €64, Private LLM | P |
| Free + one-time feature unlock | PocketPal | P |
| Local free + cloud subscription | Enclave, Anarlog | P |

- Every local-first product found with **recurring** income charges for something that has a server cost. No product was found selling a subscription for purely local processing with no server feature. (Researcher's synthesis; consistent with every verified row.)
- FUTO Voice Input, the closest Android on-device voice precedent, shows about **63K installs** on a store tracker. (U)
- No data exists anywhere on what share of users pay under an honour system.
- **Backlash cases** (Notability 2021, Voice Dream 2024): reversals followed the removal of features from people who had already paid. Charging new buyers differently (Screen Studio 2025) drew criticism but no reversal. (P)
- Paying after value has been received raises what people pay (academic work, reported by press). (P)
- **Google Play billing without a server**: Google's guidance assumes a backend; purchases must be acknowledged within three days or they are refunded; purchases restore through the buyer's Google account. Whether restore works fully offline, and whether the billing component adds an internet permission to the app, are **not verified**.
- Google Play has no mechanism for charging existing owners for a major new version; a new in-app purchase is the workaround. (U — inference)

## 8. Subscription Benchmarks (Conversion, Retention, Android Against iPhone)

Raw report: `research/04_local_first_monetization_precedents.md`. Sources: RevenueCat and Adapty 2026 reports. All **P**; the reports cover subscription apps only and say little about one-time pricing. Some figures conflict inside the reports themselves.

- Install-to-paid, freemium apps: about **2%** median at day 35; hard paywall about 10%.
- AI apps: more revenue per payer, faster churn; yearly-plan retention at 12 months about **21%** (28% across all apps).
- Android: about **15%** of subscription revenue against 85% for iPhone, with about 70% of users. Per paying user the first-year value is close ($21.62 against $23.38). The gap is in how many pay, about three times fewer.
- Median prices: $10 a month, about $35 a year.
- Productivity apps: highest refund rate of any category (4.7%); 44% of Google Play cancellations are cost-related.
- Lifetime is usually priced at several times the annual plan; about a quarter of apps offer subscription and lifetime together.

## 9. Market Size, Capable Devices & Willingness to Pay (Estimates and Surveys)

Raw report: `research/05_platform_incumbents_policy_risk_market.md`, Part C. All **U** unless marked.

- AI note-taking market: estimates of $0.6–1.1B for 2025, differing by about two times between research firms.
- Phone memory: average 8.4 GB at the end of 2025 (Counterpoint). The only breakdown found (a benchmark app's users, skewed to enthusiasts) shows 82% at 8 GB or more and 43% at 12 GB or more. **No figure exists for phones actually in use.** A 2026 memory shortage is cutting shipments and pushing mid-range phones back to 8 GB.
- Premium phones ($600+): Apple holds over 70% of units. Pixel is about 1% of global shipments; Samsung 24%.
- Willingness to pay for AI on phones (US survey, 2025): 3% would pay extra; 50% would not.
- Privacy: about 40% of US users say they worry about AI and privacy on phones. No evidence was found that "no network" has driven purchases of a voice-capture product; the commercially successful ones are cloud products.
