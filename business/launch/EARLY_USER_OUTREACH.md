## 4. Longer community post (about 150 words)

Use only where self-promotion is allowed. Read the rules first, and ask a moderator if unsure.

**Title:** I made a private voice-notes app for Android. It is new, it is paid, and I want honest feedback

**Body:**

I am one person, and I have just released an Android app called Eudemonia. I talk to myself a lot when I am thinking, and I wanted those rambles to come out as tidy notes, without sending my voice to anyone's server.

You record a thought, from a notification, a widget or the app. It is transcribed on your phone. Then a language model on the phone rewrites related thoughts as one organized note. No account, and your recordings and notes never leave the phone.

To be upfront about money: recording and transcription are free. The organizing step is free for a trial, then a one-time purchase. No subscription. It needs a phone with about 8 GB of memory and a 2.5 GB download, and it is English only.

I am looking for a few people to use it for a week and tell me what is rough. Link in the comments if that is allowed here, otherwise message me.

## 5. Five questions to ask early users after a week

1. What was the last thing you recorded, and what did you do with the note afterward?
2. Was there a moment when you wanted to record and something got in the way? What was it?
3. Open one of your organized notes. Is anything missing, wrong, or changed from what you said? What?
4. How did you start recordings most often: notification, widget or inside the app? Why that one?
5. When the trial ended (or when you imagined it ending), what did you think of the one-time price? What would have made it an easy yes or an easy no?

Optional follow-up if the answer is thin: "What would you tell a friend it does, in one sentence?" The answer shows whether the positioning has landed.

## Practical notes for the founder

- Ask each user which phone model they have. Synthesis needs about 8 GB of memory, so the answer shows who can really run it.
- Do not promise a lifetime price or free access in exchange for feedback unless you decide to, in writing, before sending.
- Keep a simple list of who you contacted, where, and when, so no one gets a second message by accident.
