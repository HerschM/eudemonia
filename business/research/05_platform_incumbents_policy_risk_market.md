# 05 - Platform Incumbents, Platform Risk and Market Size

Research date: 2026-11-24. Researcher: market-research agent. No repository files were read.

## How to read the evidence labels

- Labels on every claim: [OFFICIAL] = vendor/Google/Samsung/Apple/Nothing page or Android developer docs; [PRESS] = news/analyst coverage; [FOUNDER] = statement by a company founder/exec as reported; [ESTIMATE] = market-research firm estimate or my own inference (my inferences are marked "Inference").
- Fetch status: "(read)" = I fetched the page and read the relevant text. "(snippet)" = I only saw a search-result summary of the page (page blocked or not fetched). Treat "(snippet)" claims as lower confidence.
- Quotes marked "verbatim" were returned in quotation marks by the page fetch; they are short and should be re-checked against the live page before being used in any external document.
- NOT FOUND = I searched and could not find it. Nothing is guessed.

## Key findings (decision-relevant, 12 lines)

1. The single closest incumbent is Nothing's Essential Key + Essential Space (hardware key, long-press voice note, AI organisation). It is free, cloud-processed, and locked to Nothing/CMF phones. It has been out since March 2025. No paid tier found as of 2026-11-24.
2. Google's direct answer is Keep Live (launched 2026-09-03): speak "stream of consciousness", Gemini organises it into notes and lists. Android only, requires Google AI Plus ($4.99/mo after June 2026 price cut), Pro or Ultra. It is cloud-based and is not hardware-button-triggered (no trigger found).
3. Samsung lets you map Side-button double press to "Voice Recorder > Start recording" or "Samsung Notes > Record voice note". Galaxy AI transcribe/summarise is free ("basic features"), but a ~$4.99/mo "Galaxy AI Premium" is being tested (unannounced).
4. Google Play policy classifies "automation tools, assistants" as NOT accessibility tools. Using AccessibilityService requires a Play Console declaration, demo video, a documented Play-listing disclosure, in-app prominent disclosure and affirmative consent. "Deceptive and non-declared uses" can lead to suspension or developer-account termination.
5. Google has been tightening: Oct 30 2025 policy clarification (autonomous-agent ban); Android 17 Advanced Protection Mode (opt-in) restricts AccessibilityService to verified accessibility tools (announced ~2026-10-01). Users with Advanced Protection on cannot use the app's trigger.
6. Technical platform risk not tied to policy: Android docs say a microphone foreground service "cannot" be created while the app is in the background; the documented exemption list does not mention AccessibilityService key events. Android 17 also adds per-app memory limits (anonymous RSS + swap) and background-audio/volume-API hardening.
7. Play can host the model: Play for On-device AI (beta) allows 1.5 GB per AI pack; a ~2.5 GB model needs at least 2 packs. Packs can be targeted by device RAM.
8. Play fees changed in 2026: the new US/UK/EEA structure started 2026-06-30 (details and source conflicts in Part B).
9. Device base: AnTuTu Q1 2026 sample shows 82% of devices at 8 GB or more and 43% at 12 GB or more, but this is a benchmark-user sample, not an installed base. A 2026 DRAM crisis is shrinking shipments (-11% YoY in Q2 2026) and pushing mid-range phones from 12 GB back to 8 GB.
10. Willingness to pay for AI is weak in consumer surveys (US 2025: 3-6% would pay extra; 11% upgraded for AI). Yet a hardware voice-capture company (Plaud) reports 2M devices and ~$100M software ARR, and a dictation startup (Wispr) raised at $2B. Voice capture is monetisable, but mostly through cloud products.
11. Pixel is about 1.1% of global shipments (IDC Q2 2026); Samsung is 24%, Apple 20% (Counterpoint Q2 2026). Android is not where premium units concentrate: Apple holds over 70% of $600+ units (snippet).
12. NOT FOUND: any active-installed-base share of Android phones with 8 GB / 12 GB RAM by country; Play status of Button Mapper / Key Mapper / Tasker / MacroDroid in 2026 (Play pages blocked); AccessibilityService approvals rate; Android vs iOS ARPU for productivity.

## Part B: Google Play & Android Policy Risk (Accessibility, Foreground Service, Sideloading, Fees)

### B1. AccessibilityService on Google Play for apps that are not accessibility tools

Policy page: "Permissions and APIs that Access Sensitive Information", AccessibilityService section (read): https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16585319 [OFFICIAL]. Page shows "(c) 2026 Google" and no publication date; the Accessibility section carries no effective date. Older form of the same policy: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10964491?hl=en [OFFICIAL] (read).

Verbatim quotes (short):
- "Google Play restricts the use of the AccessibilityAPI and `isAccessibilityTool` flag, enforces feature bans," (answer/16585319)
- "Apps with a core functionality intended to directly support people with disabilities are eligible" (answer/16585319)
- "All other apps using the API must explicitly document usage on their Google Play listing," "provide prominent in-app disclosures, and secure affirmative user consent." (answer/16585319)
- "The Accessibility API cannot be used to: ... Work around Android built-in platform security controls, privacy controls and notifications" (answer/16585319)
- "The Accessibility API is not designed and cannot be requested for: remote call audio recording; an app that autonomously initiates, plans, and executes actions or decisions" (answer/16585319)
- "Apps must use more narrowly scoped APIs and permissions in lieu of the Accessibility API when possible" (answer/16585319)
- "Provide a clear Play Console declaration and demo video if using AccessibilityService API." (answer/16585319, "Key considerations")
- "Other examples of apps that are not accessibility tools are: antivirus software, automation tools, assistants" (answer/10964491). The page also lists monitoring apps, cleaners, password managers, launchers.
- Disclosure: "Must be within the app itself, cannot only be in the app description or on a website;" (answer/10964491)
- "deterministic, rule-based automation, where behavior follows a static, human-defined script" is not prohibited (answer/10964491).
- "Deceptive and non-declared uses of these APIs may result in a suspension of your app and/or termination of your developer account" (answer/10964491; wording completed from the search-result summary of the same page).
- Oct 30 2025 policy announcement (read): "any use of this API that enables an app to autonomously initiate, plan, and execute actions is prohibited" - framed as clarification; developers had "at least 30 days from October 30, 2025" to comply. https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16550159 [OFFICIAL]
- Declaration requirement history: from Nov 3 2021, apps targeting Android 12+ with an AccessibilityService must complete a Play Console declaration (answer/10964491; also Android Developers Blog, 2021-07, snippet): https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2021/07/announcing-policy-updates-to-bolster.html [OFFICIAL].
- Upcoming policy changes (read): none dated 2026; 2027 items cover location button, contacts, AppFunctions, local network. No upcoming accessibility change listed. https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13986130 [OFFICIAL]

What this means for a volume-chord trigger (Inference):
- Eudemonia is not an "accessibility tool" under the stated test (the page says a general voice assistant that happens to help some motor-impaired users does not qualify; snippet of the fetch summary of answer/10964491). It must therefore file the declaration, record a demo video, document the use on the Play listing, and show the in-app disclosure plus affirmative consent. Setting `isAccessibilityTool=true` without qualifying is itself a prohibited misdeclaration.
- A chord trigger is deterministic and does not "autonomously initiate, plan, and execute" anything, so the October 2025 clause should not apply to it. Inference: the residual review risk is a reviewer classifying the app as an "assistant" and asking why no "narrower API" works. For a screen-off two-key chord I know of no narrower API; the argument would have to be made in the declaration. NOT FOUND: any Play statement on volume-key filtering specifically.

### B2. Android restricted settings, Advanced Protection and Android 17

- Restricted settings (Android 13+). Google Help (read): "some settings are locked when you install an app" and "can't be changed unless you allow restricted settings"; accessibility is called out; path "Settings > Apps > [app] > More > Allow restricted settings"; "We don't recommend that you allow restricted settings unless you trust the app developer." https://support.google.com/android/answer/12623953 [OFFICIAL]. Android 15 widened this to more permissions and treats an app as "sideloaded" if installed by an app that did not use the store-grade install API (snippet): https://androidauthority.com/android-15-restricted-settings-sideloading-3481098 [PRESS]. Effect for a Play-installed app: not restricted (Inference from the Help text that applies to apps from outside Google Play; the page itself does not address Play installs directly). For a GitHub/F-Droid APK: extra friction, documented by Key Mapper's own guide https://docs.keymapper.club/user-guide/restricted-settings/ (returned 404 when I tried it).
- Advanced Protection Mode (APM) and accessibility (Android 17). ### B3. Foreground service, microphone and background recording

Android platform rules (read): https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/fgs/service-types [OFFICIAL]
- Microphone type needs `FOREGROUND_SERVICE_MICROPHONE` and the `RECORD_AUDIO` runtime permission. Verbatim: "you cannot create a `microphone` foreground service while your app is in the background and you cannot launch a `microphone` foreground service from a `BOOT_COMPLETED` receiver, with a few exceptions." Description: "Continue microphone capture from the background, such as voice recorders or communication apps."
- Background start rules (read): https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/fgs/restrictions-bg-start [OFFICIAL]. Verbatim: while-in-use services (microphone, camera, location) "cannot create the service while the app is in the background, even if the app falls into one of the exemptions from background start restrictions". Exemptions from the while-in-use rule listed: a system component starts it; app widgets; notification interaction; a `PendingIntent` from a different visible app; device-owner DPC; an app that provides `VoiceInteractionService`; privileged `START_ACTIVITIES_FROM_BACKGROUND`. Not listed: AccessibilityService key events, battery-optimisation exemption, or `SYSTEM_ALERT_WINDOW` (the last two are only exemptions from the generic background-start restriction, not from the while-in-use rule). NOT FOUND: any statement that an AccessibilityService key event allows creating a microphone FGS. Inference: the product only works with the screen off if the microphone-capable service is already running, or is started from a listed exemption, or the app holds the voice-interaction role; the engineering team should verify this on Android 14, 15, 16 and 17 across OEMs because a regression would break the core promise.
- Android 17 "Background audio hardening" (read): audio framework restricts "audio playback, audio focus requests, and volume change APIs" in the background; for apps targeting API 37 "the foreground service must have while-in-use (WIU) capabilities". https://developer.android.com/about/versions/17/behavior-changes-all [OFFICIAL]. Relevance: if the volume-key pass-through calls volume-change APIs from the accessibility service, test this on Android 17 (Inference).
- Android 17 per-app memory limits (read): Android Developers Blog, 2026-08-19: limits first in Android 17 on Pixel, manufacturers to apply "across their portfolio of device RAM configurations from 4GB to 16GB+ devices"; on breach "the system moves its pages into compressed RAM" and if growth continues "it will be terminated"; exit reason contains "MemoryLimiter:AnonSwap"; Android vitals reports "Anonymous RSS + swap". https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2026/08/app-broader-memory-limits.html [OFFICIAL]. NOT FOUND: the actual limit values per RAM class, whether file-backed mmap model weights count, and any foreground-service exemption. Inference: a resident 2.5 GB anonymous-memory model is the kind of outlier this targets; mmap'd weights may count differently, so measure on an Android 17 Pixel with `am memory-limiter` (documented in the Android 17 behaviour-changes page).

Google Play requirements (read): https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13392821 [OFFICIAL]
- For apps targeting Android 14+, "need to declare any foreground service types that you use" on the App content page; each type needs a functionality description, user-impact description, demo-video link and use case. Microphone use case "Background Audio Access": the video must "Show the user initiate the microphone actively recording audio or receiving voice commands." Verbatim on visibility: "User perceptible means that the user should be aware that a foreground service task is running on their device."
- Inference: a hardware-button trigger from the pocket fits "user initiates recording" as long as the video shows the user pressing the chord and a persistent notification / indicator is visible. A permanently running always-listening service would not fit.
- Play target API rule: apps must "target Android 16 (API level 36) or higher" by Aug 31, 2026, with an extension to Nov 1, 2026 (read): https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/11926878 [OFFICIAL]. Date for API 37: NOT FOUND.

### B4. REQUEST_IGNORE_BATTERY_OPTIMIZATIONS

- Official Android doc (read) says verbatim: "Google Play policies prohibit apps from requesting direct exemption from Power Management features---Doze and App Standby---in Android 6.0 and above unless the core function of the app is adversely affected." https://developer.android.com/training/monitoring-device-state/doze-standby [OFFICIAL]
- The same page's acceptable-use table lists: messaging/VoIP apps only if FCM cannot be used; "Safety app"; "Task automation app. App's core function is scheduling automated actions"; "Peripheral device companion app" (persistent connection only). "In general, your app doesn't meet these exceptions unless Doze or App Standby breaks the core function of the app". A voice-capture app is not listed.
- "Most apps can invoke an intent that contains the ACTION_IGNORE_BATTERY_OPTIMIZATION_SETTINGS" (opens the settings list, no special permission), while only apps meeting an acceptable use case "can instead invoke ... ACTION_REQUEST_IGNORE_BATTERY_OPTIMIZATIONS" (same doc). Inference: the safer Play path is the settings-list intent plus in-app guidance; declaring the `REQUEST_IGNORE_BATTERY_OPTIMIZATIONS` permission needs a core-function argument that Doze breaks screen-off capture, and the likeliest accepted category is "task automation".
- The Play help page for the sensitive-permissions policy I fetched (answer/9888170) did not contain this permission, and I could not locate the Play Console declaration form text. NOT FOUND: Play's own wording and any list of enforcement cases in 2025-2026. An old 2015 developer report of a removal over this permission exists (snippet): https://redlib.ssps.io/r/androiddev/comments/3s9ggu/psa_declaring_request_ignore_battery/cwvjpvd/ [PRESS].
- Samsung's own Voice Recorder page says recordings "may stop when the screen locks" unless battery is set to Unrestricted (read, A3), evidence that even first-party recording is vulnerable on Samsung.

### B7. Hosting a ~2.5 GB model on Play

- Size limits (read): https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9859372 [OFFICIAL]: base module 500 MB; individual asset packs 1.5 GB; install-time total 4 GB; on-demand/fast-follow asset packs cumulative 30 GB; overall max 34 GB; apps over 200 MB show a non-blocking install warning on mobile data; "Apps larger than 1GB must target the minimum SDK version of Android Lollipop (API level 21) or higher."
- Play for On-device AI (beta) (read): https://developer.android.com/google/play/on-device-ai [OFFICIAL]. Delivery: "install-time, fast-follow, or on-demand"; "Individual AI packs can be up to 1.5GB, based on their compressed download sizes. The maximum cumulative app size of any version of your app generated from your app bundle is 4GB."; "AI packs only contain models. Java, Kotlin, and native libraries are not allowed"; "Models downloaded by Play for On-device AI should only be used by your apps"; device targeting by "Device RAM" with `device_ram min_bytes/max_bytes`; "It's not possible to prevent any variant of your AI pack being delivered to certain devices. Non-targeted devices will always receive the default variant" (the default can be empty). Downloads over 200 MB off Wi-Fi need user consent. Play AI Delivery Library is `0.1.1-alpha01` in the doc sample.
- Conflict: the AI-pack page says a 4 GB cumulative cap; the general size page allows 30 GB cumulative for fast-follow/on-demand packs. Both are official; which applies to AI packs is unclear. Inference: a 2.5 GB model fits as two AI packs (each under 1.5 GB) within 4 GB either way; a 4B model plus a 6 GB-tier smaller model in one bundle could breach 4 GB if both are install-time.
- A third-party write-up reports a ~2.4 GB Gemma model must be split into parts under 1,500 MB (snippet): https://ringringdeven.blogspot.com/2026/05/delivering-large-llms-via-google-play.html [PRESS].
- Design relevance (Inference): delivering weights through Play AI packs avoids an app-owned model download server and lets the manifest omit `INTERNET`, which supports the "zero network traffic" claim and a clean Data Safety form.
