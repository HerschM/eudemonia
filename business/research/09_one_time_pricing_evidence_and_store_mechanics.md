# Brief 09 — One-Time ("Lifetime") Pricing: Comparable Prices, Conversion Evidence, Trial Design & Store Mechanics (Google Play and Apple App Store)

> Raw research report. Not authoritative until promoted into `COMPETITIVE_LANDSCAPE.md`. Facts only, no recommendations.
> Researcher: `eudemonia-market-researcher`. Research date: **2026-11-25** (all "fetched" dates are this date unless stated).
> Grades: `[OFFICIAL]` company's own site, store listing or platform documentation. `[PRESS]` news or reference-site reporting. `[FOUNDER]` developer's own post, forum post or interview. `[ESTIMATE]` third-party estimate, vendor blog or aggregator.
> "NOT FOUND" means searched and not located. Nothing here is filled from memory.

---

## 0. Method Limits That Affect Every Table (Read First)

1. **Google Play listings could not be read.** Fetches of `play.google.com` pages return only a page header (JS-rendered), so **no Android store price was verified from a Play listing.** Android prices below come only from developers' own sites or third-party articles and are graded accordingly. "Android versus iPhone price" is therefore mostly NOT FOUND.
2. **Apple App Store listings were readable** (`apps.apple.com`, US and India storefronts), but an app's "In-App Purchases" list is cumulative: it shows every product ever created (legacy tiers, regional tiers, duplicates with the same name) without saying which is current. Where a listing is ambiguous I give the full list or a range and say so. I did not guess which row is which except where noted as "inference".
3. Many official pricing pages carry no date beyond a "© 2026" footer. I state that when it is the case.
4. Several pages returned HTTP 403 (Indie Hackers, Plex support, Business Wire, Morningstar, The Desk). Facts from those pages come from search-result snippets and are marked "snippet, page not opened".

### 2.4 RevenueCat Hard Paywall Versus Freemium (10.7% vs 2.1%) — Confirmed, With Definition and a Conflict

- **Confirmed.** "Apps that ask for money upfront convert 5x better than freemium (10.7% vs. 2.1%)." Hard paywall D35 median **10.7%**, top quartile above 20.0%; freemium D35 median **2.1%**, top quartile above 4.5%. https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps/ [OFFICIAL] 2026-11-25.
- **Definition on that page.** "Download-to-paid (D35): the share of installs that result in at least one paid subscription within 35 days of the install date." Access method: "how users primarily access the app (freemium, via a hard paywall)". No separate definition of "hard paywall"; the closest description is "whether users hit a paywall the moment they open your app, or get to explore first."
- **Conflict in RevenueCat's own wording.** The blog article (https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/subscription-app-trends-benchmarks-2026 [OFFICIAL], 2026-03-19) states "Day 35 trial-to-paid conversion" in the body but "Day 35 download-to-paid" in a chart caption. The report page says download-to-paid.
- **Scope.** The metric is **paid subscriptions** among RevenueCat's apps (115,000+ apps, $16B revenue per the report). It is not a one-time-purchase metric.
- **Related.** 2025 values reported as 12.1% (hard paywall) vs 2.2% (freemium) in secondary coverage (https://ppc.land/ai-apps-earn-41-more-per-user-but-churn-30-faster-revenuecat-finds/ [PRESS], not re-checked against the 2025 report). Median refund rate: hard paywall 2.5% vs freemium 2.9% (RevenueCat SoSA 2026 page). Price-tier D35 download-to-paid: high-priced 2.8%, mid 2.0%, low 1.4% (already established).

## 3. Trial Design for a One-Time Purchase (Question 3)

### 3.1 What Apple Officially Provides

Apple App Review Guidelines, section 3.1.1, fetched 2026-11-25 from https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/ [OFFICIAL] (no revision date in the text read):

> "Non-subscription apps may offer a free time-based trial period before presenting a full unlock option by setting up a Non-Consumable IAP item at Price Tier 0 that follows the naming convention: "XX-day Trial." Prior to the start of the trial, your app must clearly identify its duration, the content or services that will no longer be accessible when the trial ends, and any downstream charges the user would need to pay for full functionality. Learn more about managing content access and the duration of the trial period using Receipts and DeviceCheck."

Apple DTS (Developer Technical Support) 2026 position, as quoted by a developer in the Apple Developer Forums thread "Free trial for one-time purchase: Is the $0 IAP workaround still recommended in 2026?" https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/823270 [FOUNDER relaying Apple], Apr–May 2026:
> "Yes this is the current approach to allow free app download with functionality gated by a free and paid non-consumable IAP purchase."
> "With originalPurchaseDate the users clock would start immediately upon purchase time… a free non-consumable enables the users to know the terms and start the clock when they are ready."

The poster notes App Store Connect "does not officially support trials for paid apps" and that "handling refunds, reinstalls, activation timing, and purchase history requires non-obvious logic". Apple did not comment on refunds, reinstalls or Family Sharing in that thread. A 2023 report (https://mjtsai.com/blog/2023/05/09/app-store-rules-regarding-trials [PRESS]) records a developer rejection over a trial and another developer saying Apple "is fine with apps that offer zero functionality after trial expires"; both are developer experience, not Apple statements.

### 3.2 What Google Play Provides

- **No built-in free trial for one-time products: not stated in one sentence by Google; confirmed by absence.** Google's one-time product documentation lists purchase options "Buy" and "Rent", and offers "Pre-order" (buy only) and "Discount" (buy and rent); the Play Console help adds a "Play Games reward offer". ## 5. Google Play One-Time Product Mechanics (Question 5)

### 5.1 Price Experiments

Source: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13343030?hl=en [OFFICIAL] 2026-11-25 (page undated, "©2026 Google"). Original announcement: https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2023/05/price-experiments-in-play-console.html [OFFICIAL] 2023-05.

- Works **only for active one-time products**; you must be a merchant developer.
- Up to **two price variants plus a control**; up to 1,000 purchase options per experiment; **one experiment per country/region at a time**; app-level; **cannot be paused, only stopped**; the price of a product in a running experiment cannot be changed.
- Variant change: decrease 1–99% or increase 1–999%, whole numbers.
- **Minimum detectable effect:** default 30%, selectable 5%–50% in 5% steps, fixed after launch. Smaller effect = longer run.
- **Confidence level:** default 90%, selectable 70%–99%, fixed at launch. Significance is reached "when the interval doesn't include zero".
- **Duration:** estimated time to significance is shown from historical data (inaccurate for new products with no history); maximum run **6 months**; the experiment ends automatically **14 days after** a result is determined; a new experiment with overlapping products and countries cannot start until **30 days** after the previous one ends.
- **Minimum sample: NOT stated.** The Console warns when predicted volume is too low; with very low volumes "no data may appear". Suggested fixes: add countries, test more products, use one variant, increase audience, raise the MDE, lower the confidence level. Google's help says two-variant experiments need more time than one-variant ones.
- **Audience:** 1–100% of users, split equally across control and variants; users outside the experiment see the control price and are excluded from analysis.
- Long experiments can be affected by currency fluctuation; an inconclusive result may mean buyers are indifferent to the price difference.
- **What developers report:** only a Google-published testimonial from a small developer ("set up experiments in minutes", "makes a great tool for small developers") in the 2023 blog. No independent developer report on inconclusive results or low-volume behaviour found.

### 5.2 Regional Pricing and Templates

Source: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6334373?hl=en [OFFICIAL] 2026-11-25.
- "Play uses the price you enter as the base for calculating market-specific prices"; Play converts to local currency, adds tax in select countries, applies "local pricing patterns and the exchange rate on the date you set the price".
- **Pricing templates no longer manage product prices:** "Starting October 27th, 2025, you'll no longer be able to manage prices across your products using pricing templates." Existing templates were unlinked; changes are per product. For one-time products, regional prices are set per purchase option (the page section applies "only if you have enrolled for the one-time product program").
- Number of countries: NOT FOUND (page links to a list of price ranges by country).
- Discount offers on one-time products can have country eligibility, a purchase limit, a limited time window and a quantity limit (https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/one-time-product-multi-purchase-options-offers [OFFICIAL]).

### 5.3 Promo Codes for One-Time Products

Source: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6321495?hl=en [OFFICIAL] 2026-11-25.
- "You can create up to 500 promo codes per quarter across all non-subscription promotions in an app." Unused codes do not carry over. Only active buy purchase options qualify. One-time-use codes redeemable "directly from the Google Play or in-app". Promotions can last up to one year; the code count cannot be changed after creation; codes are delivered as a CSV. Which countries support promo codes: NOT FOUND.
- Purchases made with promo codes do not generate an orderId (https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/security [OFFICIAL] 2026-11-25).

### 5.4 Refunds

- **Within 48 hours:** a user can request a refund through Google Play "depending on the details of the purchase"; after 48 hours "The developer can help with purchase issues and can process refunds according to their policies and applicable laws." https://support.google.com/googleplay/answer/15574908?hl=en [OFFICIAL] 2026-11-25 (undated). The 2-hour window applies to **paid apps**, not in-app purchases (search snippet of Google help; the page above does not mention it).
- **EEA and UK:** for in-app digital content "you waive your automatic statutory right of withdrawal" at purchase unless content is "defective, unavailable, or doesn't perform as stated" (a 14-day withdrawal right applies to digital services). https://support.google.com/googleplay/answer/15576634?hl=en [OFFICIAL] 2026-11-25.
- **Developer-side:** refunds can be issued in Play Console by Order ID; the Orders:refund API with `revoke` is the documented way to refund and revoke an illegitimate purchase (https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/security [OFFICIAL] 2026-11-25).
- **Acknowledgement window:** "purchases are refunded after 3 days if your app has not processed the purchase" (https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/integrate [OFFICIAL] 2026-11-25).
- **Who bears a refund's cost:** a 9to5Google report (search snippet, date unconfirmed) says refunds are subtracted from developer payouts; treat as possibly outdated [PRESS].

### 5.5 Verifying a Purchase, and the INTERNET Permission Question

- **Google's documentation does not state whether the app needs the INTERNET permission to use Play Billing.** I found no Google page that says INTERNET is required or not required. The integration guide (Play Billing Library **9.1.0**, released 2026-06-18 per https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/release-notes [OFFICIAL]) never mentions the INTERNET permission.
- **Local versus server verification (Google).** The purchase flow lists "Verify the purchase on your server" as a step. "Your apps can store the purchase token locally, though we strongly recommend passing the token to your secure backend server". Server verification uses the Play Developer API (`Purchases.products:get`). Client-side acknowledge is allowed; server-side gives "additional protection against issues like poor network connectivity". https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/integrate and https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/security [OFFICIAL] 2026-11-25. `queryPurchasesAsync()` is recommended on each launch to handle network loss, multi-device and out-of-app purchases (same guide). Google documentation on **offline behaviour or a local purchase cache: NOT FOUND**.
- **Where INTERNET comes from (developer reports, not Google):**
  - A developer's research brief in a public GitHub issue (opened 2026-10-15, closed 2026-11-24): "The billing AAR's own manifest does not ask for INTERNET"; INTERNET and ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE arrive "transitively through datatransport" (`transport-backend-cct:3.1.8`) with `billing-ktx:9.1.0`; "Stripping the permissions is undocumented"; the INTERNET-removed build was build-checked only, **not tested on a device**; "Current official docs say nothing about offline behavior or the cache"; the owner shipped with INTERNET. https://github.com/alexandre-machado/audio-blackbox/issues/441 [FOUNDER] 2026-10-15 to 2026-11-24.
  - A Flutter developer's post (dated "Nov 21", year not shown): "Billing still works without `INTERNET`: the library talks to the Play Store app, and the Play Store does the networking"; author comment: the permission comes from Google's datatransport library that Billing pulls in. https://dev.to/techiejester/shipping-a-flutter-app-with-no-internet-permission-the-plugins-that-tried-to-add-it-back-n4b [FOUNDER].
  - Reddit, 2024-07-16: "PSA: Play Billing Library v6 silently adds the" internet permissions to the merged manifest; commenters say billing does not need it and report removal with a manifest override (`tools:node="remove"`); community-reported only. https://lr.eu.psf.lt/r/androiddev/comments/1e4ctxd/psa_play_billing_library_v6_silently_adds_the/lde6kls/?context=3 [FOUNDER] (search snippet).
  - ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE also arrives transitively; the GitHub brief calls removing it "probably not safe on Android 14+" (its own inference).
- Net: **community evidence says billing works without INTERNET in the app, but no Google statement was found and no device test of purchase, acknowledge, restore and refund flows without the permission was found.**
